3. Standard wire diameters

The heat map compares mean coil diameter D (rows) with the standard wire diameters (columns). Colour follows corrected stress τk as a percentage of allowable; grey cells fail a pocket, index, solid-length or coil-count check. Tap a cell to select that D and d.

  • Well within (τk ≤ 70 % of allowable)
  • Approaching or at the limit (70–100 %)
  • Over allowable (τk > 100 %)
  • Invalid (pocket, index 4–16, solid length or coil count)

Swipe the map sideways to see every standard wire. Highlighted rows give the most viable wire options.

Each size is also listed below, evaluated at the largest mean diameter that still fits the pocket: D = De max − d. Corrected stress τk uses the Bergsträsser factor from BS EN 13906-1.

4. Optimise mean coil diameter

For the selected wire, vary D between the index limits (4 to 16) and the pocket. Smaller D lowers stress but needs more coils and a longer solid length.

Stress is the curve labelled “Stress % of allowable”. Solid length is the curve labelled “Solid length Lc”. The dashed line labelled L2 is the shorter working length; solid length must stay below it. Colour supports the labels and is not the only cue.

Method, formulae and limitations

Calculations follow the BS EN 13906-1 method for cylindrical helical compression springs of round wire. Manufacturing tolerances follow BS EN 15800 (quality grades 1, 2 and 3), which replaced BS 1726 for cylindrical helical compression spring tolerances. The designer remains responsible for checking the current standards, the material certificate, fatigue if the duty is cyclic, buckling, relaxation and manufacture.

  • Spring index w = D / d
  • Bergsträsser factor k = (w + 0.5) / (w − 0.75)
  • Uncorrected shear stress τ = 8 D F / (π d³)
  • Corrected stress τk = k τ
  • Rate R = G d⁴ / (8 D³ n)
  • Allowable static screening stress τzul = factor × Rm (default factor 0.5)
  • Closed and ground ends: nt = n + 2, Lc = nt × d

Coil-diameter tolerances are taken from EN 15800 Table 3. Force and length tolerances use the grade factor Q, the coil factor kf and the geometry factor aF. Q, kf, the force and length formulae, squareness, parallelism and the scope limits were checked against published clause values. Table 3 and the aF coefficients were transcribed from a public copy of the standard and are flagged in the results because they were not re-read from a licensed PDF.
